I called the film as panel A instantly and with total confidence. In A the room IS architecture: colossal piers press in on both sides at the board's edge, fill the frame top to bottom, blow past the top of frame into unresolved void, and the ranks are backed by more stone crowding right behind them — you cannot see where the room ends. In our render (B) the entire chamber is visible at once: a small three-arch wall roughly 20 m back, flat empty tiled floor wrapping the board on all four sides, and nothing whatsoever in the left and right thirds, so the board reads as a lit diorama on a table in an empty warehouse rather than a cathedral-scale hall. The masonry idiom is also the wrong one and the brief flags it explicitly as a discarded claim: we have crude coursed rectangular blocks with heavy dark mortar joints and plain semicircular Romanesque voussoirs, where the film has smooth ordered carved stone — concentric stepped portal mouldings, a geometric relief door, a fine blind arcade — and our block size is so oversized the arch reads as ~4 m against pieces that should dwarf a child. I identified the film as panel A correctly, at a glance, with high confidence — this was not a close call. The single defining element of the reference frame is a column of dense opaque grey-white dust with real internal lobes, soft self-shadowed rolls and a fine trailing veil, occupying the centre third of frame; our render substitutes a rigid pile of hard-edged white facets and clips them to pure white, which is why fracBlown reads 0.0551 against the film's 0.0003 and detailTotal reads 0.0239 against 0.0086 — we have nearly three times the reference's edge energy where the film has almost none, because dust has no edges.
